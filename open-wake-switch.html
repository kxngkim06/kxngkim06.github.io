Open Wake Switch

Discovery Project, in progress

The problem

I wanted a light that turns on by itself at a set time in the morning. Smart switches do this, but they mean removing the wall switch and handling the wiring behind it. I wanted a device that leaves the wall untouched and simply presses the existing switch with a servo, so it could work in any room without electrical work.

The process

I started with an ESP32-WROVER board and a small servo, programmed in the Arduino IDE. The servo signal comes from GPIO18. My first goal was simple: a test sketch that rotates the servo 30 degrees every second, with serial output so I could watch what the board was doing.

That test taught me the most. The serial monitor showed the sketch running, but the servo wasn't moving. Because the code looked correct, I first assumed the board was the problem. Instead of rewriting code, I connected the servo directly to the ESP32 pins with jumper wires, skipping the breadboard. It moved immediately. The fault was a bad connection on the breadboard, which serial output could never have revealed.

In parallel, I looked at the mechanical side. I examined the screws on the switch cover and I'm designing a removable bracket that holds the servo in place, so the device can be attached and taken off without damaging the wall plate.

The outcome so far

The servo now runs reliably in the prototype, and I've confirmed the wiring and the control pin. The lesson is one I expect to reuse often: when software and hardware disagree, test the hardware directly before changing the code.

What's next

Still to do: add time-based scheduling so the servo moves at a chosen morning time, finish the mounting bracket, test it on a real wall switch, and run repeated trials to measure reliability. I'll update this page as each piece is finished.
