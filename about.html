About me

I didn't pick electrical engineering for a grand reason. I was strong in math and science, I knew I wanted to be an engineer, and EE looked like a practical path. What changed things was the work itself. Once I started building, I found that the most exciting part was using software to control and command hardware. That curiosity pulled me toward robotics and embedded systems, and it's why I'm building projects like Open Wake Switch today.

Learning from testing

On Auburn's Design/Build/Fly team, I helped choose a propeller by testing three candidates on a thrust stand, then again in moving air at 45, 55, and 65 mph on a rig mounted to a car roof. Real airflow showed what the bench numbers hid: one propeller led on the stand, and another pulled ahead at higher speeds. That habit of verifying a design against reality is the one I try to carry into every project.

Teaching

I've taught since 2024, first as a private tutor and then at MK Academy, where I built SAT Math and Algebra curricula. Explaining ideas clearly to students has made me a better engineer, because I can't explain something I only half understand.

Where I am now

I transferred from Auburn University to Georgia Tech in 2026. This past summer I interned at TPOINT, wiring industrial control panels and setting up robots on a production line. I'm still learning to code well, and I'm finding that building real devices is the fastest way to do it.
